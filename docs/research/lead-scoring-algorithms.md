## 1. CRM lead-scoring methodologies

**Classic points-based scoring (HubSpot/Marketo tradition).** Additive model: `Score = Σ points(attribute) + Σ points(action) − Σ penalties`, with a threshold that flips a lead to MQL. Attributes split into demographic/firmographic (title, industry, company size) and behavioral (form fills, email opens, page views); HubSpot's manual scoring explicitly supports **positive and negative attributes** — negatives like "unsubscribed" or "wrong industry" subtract points ([HubSpot lead scoring guide](https://www.default.com/post/hubspot-lead-scoring), [Hightouch on HubSpot scoring](https://hightouch.com/blog/lead-scoring-and-account-scoring-in-hubspot), [MarketVeep guide](https://www.marketveep.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-lead-scoring-in-hubspot)). Two conventions worth noting beyond the raw sum: *score decay* (points expire if no activity for N days — a discrete cousin of our half-life) and *threshold bands* (hot/warm/cold labels on score ranges).

**Lead grading vs scoring — the two-axis model (Salesforce/Pardot).** The industry's most durable structural idea: **score = engagement/interest (0–100+, dynamic), grade = fit vs ideal-customer profile (A–F, semi-static)** — kept as *separate* numbers, never blended into one ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/pinpoint-hot-prospects-with-blended-pardot-scoring-and-grading/), [Interrupt Media](https://interruptmedia.com/pardot-scoring-vs-grading-whats-the-difference/), [Pardot School](https://pardotschool.com/why-pardot-scoring-grading-are-better-together/)). The MQL trigger is a conjunction, e.g. `score ≥ 50 AND grade ≥ B`. The four quadrants (high fit/high engagement = route now; high fit/low engagement = nurture; low fit/high engagement = researcher/job-hunter noise; low/low = disqualify) are the standard operator mental model. Practitioner claim: engagement-only scoring causes sales to reject 60–70% of MQLs as "not a fit" ([Solutions4SF](https://solutions4sf.com/blog/pardot-lead-scoring-grading-2026/)).

**RFM (recency–frequency–monetary).** From direct-mail/database marketing: compute per customer `R = days since last interaction`, `F = interaction count in window`, `M = spend in window`; then **quintile-score each dimension 1–5 relative to the tenant's own population** (top 20% most recent = R5, etc.) and concatenate (R=5,F=4,M=5 → "545") or sum ([Putler RFM guide](https://www.putler.com/rfm-analysis/), [Omniconvert RFM model](https://www.omniconvert.com/blog/rfm-model/), [Rejoiner](https://www.rejoiner.com/resources/rfm-analysis)). The two transferable ideas: (a) frequency as a first-class signal distinct from recency; (b) **rank/quantile normalization against the tenant's own distribution** instead of absolute thresholds — self-calibrating per tenant. Variants: weighted RFM (per-tenant weights on R/F/M), RFE (engagement replaces monetary for non-transactional businesses).

**Qualification frameworks as structured-fit checklists.** BANT = Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline; CHAMP = Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization (buyer-pain-first reordering of BANT); MEDDIC = Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion ([Salesforce BANT vs MEDDIC](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales/bant-vs-meddic/), [SPOTIO comparison](https://spotio.com/blog/b2b-sales-qualification-frameworks/), [Leads at Scale](https://leadsatscale.com/insights/b2b-lead-qualification-framework-bant-vs-champ-vs-meddic/)). Rule of thumb in the field: BANT for SMB/short cycles, MEDDIC for enterprise/long cycles. These are not algorithms — they are **named checklist vocabularies**: each item is a boolean/tri-state fact with an obvious one-line reason string ("Budget: confirmed", "Champion: none identified"), which is exactly the shape of a structured-fit component.

**Predictive lead scoring — what the vendors actually use.**

- *Salesforce Einstein*: trains on the tenant's historical **lead field data plus activity data**, finds what converted leads had in common, builds per-segment models, refreshes every ~10 days, rescores every ~6 hours. Notably it does deterministic-ish feature engineering first — canonicalizing job titles into Job Rank (C-level/VP/…) and Department — and its UI shows each lead's **"top positives" and "top negatives"** field values ([Salesforce help: how Einstein scores leads](https://help.salesforce.com/apex/HTViewHelpDoc?id=einstein_sales_els_how_it_works.htm), [Trailhead: Einstein scoring](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/lead-scoring-and-grading-in-account-engagement/einstein-scoring-in-account-engagement)). That reasons display is industry validation of the one-reason-per-signal design.
- *HubSpot predictive*: ML over historical contact data; sold alongside, not instead of, manual scoring — both scores live in separate properties ([HubSpot community on manual vs predictive](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Lists-Lead-Scoring-Workflows/Manual-vs-Predictive-Lead-Scoring/td-p/3012)).
- *MadKudu*: explicitly a **"glass box" dual model — Fit (firmographics, role, industry) × Intent/Likelihood-to-buy (behavioral, product usage)** — showing which signals drove the number ([MadKudu predictive engine](https://www.madkudu.com/predictive-engine), [SyncGTM review](https://syncgtm.com/blog/madkudu-review)). Closest commercial analogue to this scorer's philosophy.
- *6sense*: predictive models over third-party intent data + firmographics + engagement, account-level ABM focus ([BuildMVPFast overview](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/ai-lead-scoring-clearbit-6sense-predictive-analytics-2026)). Its differentiator is *purchased intent data* — off the table under the no-purchased-data constraint; the useful residue is only its fit/intent/recency signal taxonomy.

**Credit scorecards (WOE + points-to-double-odds) — the deterministic, reasons-first scoring tradition.** Banking solved "explainable additive score from outcome data" decades ago. Bin each variable; per bin compute **Weight of Evidence** `WOE_i = ln( %good_i / %bad_i )` and variable strength **Information Value** `IV = Σ_i (%good_i − %bad_i) × WOE_i`; fit logistic regression on WOE features; then scale to points with `Factor = PDO / ln(2)` and `Offset = TargetScore − Factor × ln(TargetOdds)` so that final `Score = Offset + Factor × ln(odds)` decomposes into **integer points per variable-bin** — every lead's score is a sum of named, auditable line items ([scorecard walkthrough](https://lelesgaray.github.io/blog/scorecard/), [OptBinning](https://github.com/guillermo-navas-palencia/optbinning)). This is the strongest "elaborate on what we have" candidate: it is precisely a data-tuned points-based model with a readable reason per bin.

## 2. Platform ranking algorithms adaptable to lead ranking

**Facebook EdgeRank (pre-2011).** `E = Σ_edges u_e × w_e × d_e` — **affinity** (strength of the viewer↔creator relationship, built from past interactions) × **weight** (per interaction-type value: comment > like, video > text) × **time decay** ([Wikipedia: EdgeRank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EdgeRank), [Practical Ecommerce explainer](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/Understanding-EdgeRank-Facebook-s-Quality-Score-for-Wall-Posts)). Two ideas transfer: (a) *multiplicative composition* — a zero on any factor zeroes the term (our dealbreakers are already this, generalized); (b) **affinity as an accumulated per-relationship multiplier** — a lead who has interacted with the tenant before scores higher on every subsequent signal, and affinity itself decays.

**Google PageRank.** `PR(A) = (1 − d) + d × Σ_i PR(T_i)/C(T_i)`, damping `d = 0.85`, iterated to convergence — authority flows along graph edges ([Brin & Page 1998, original paper](http://infolab.stanford.edu/pub/papers/google.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PageRank)). Deterministic, fully explainable ("scored up because referred by X, who converted"). For leads this needs a *graph*: referral edges, same-company/same-domain edges, shared-engagement edges. High ceiling, but a dependency-heavy signal — only worth it once relationship edges exist in the data model.

**YouTube's watch-time/velocity era (2012–2015).** In October 2012 YouTube switched ranking from view *count* to **time watched** — reward depth of engagement, not raw event tally ([Search Engine Watch, Oct 2012](https://www.searchenginewatch.com/2012/10/22/youtube-algorithm-change-time-watched-key-to-higher-video-search-rankings/)); alongside it, **view velocity** — the rate of engagement immediately after publication — as a promotion trigger ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm/), [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2023/09/15/youtube-algorithm)). Lead translation: (a) weight *depth* signals (reply, meeting booked, docs read end-to-end) above *count* signals (opens); (b) a **surge/velocity flag**: signals-per-day in a trailing window vs the lead's lifetime baseline — "this lead is heating up *now*."

**Hacker News gravity.** `Score = (P − 1) / (T + 2)^G`, points P, age-in-hours T, gravity `G ≈ 1.8` ([Salihefendic, "How Hacker News ranking algorithm works"](https://medium.com/hacking-and-gonzo/how-hacker-news-ranking-algorithm-works-1d9b0cf2c08d)) — plus a **penalty system** of multiplicative demotions for flagged/controversial items ([Ken Shirriff's analysis](http://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html)). Takeaways: power-law decay is an alternative shape to the exponential half-life (heavier tail — old-but-strong leads die slower); HN penalties = **soft dealbreakers** — multiplicative downweights (×0.2) for "suspicious but not disqualifying," a middle rung between full weight and hard zero.

**Reddit hot.** `Score = log10(max(|ups − downs|, 1)) + sign(ups − downs) × (t_seconds − 1134028003) / 45000` ([algorithm walkthrough](https://medium.com/jp-tech/how-are-popular-ranking-algorithms-such-as-reddit-and-hacker-news-working-724e639ed9f7)). The durable idea is the **log-compression of counts**: the 10th repeat of the same signal type is worth far less than the 1st — directly applicable to any frequency counter so one spammy behavior can't saturate a component.

**Wilson score lower bound.** Rank small-sample binary rates by the *lower bound* of the 95% confidence interval, not the raw average ([Evan Miller, "How Not To Sort By Average Rating"](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html)):

```
LB = ( p̂ + z²/2n − z·√[ (p̂(1−p̂) + z²/4n) / n ] ) / ( 1 + z²/n )
```

with `p̂ = positives/n`, `z = 1.96`. Deterministic, one formula, famously used for Reddit's "best" comment sort. Use anywhere a component is a rate estimated from n events (reply rate, per-segment conversion rate, "does this fit-rule predict wins") — 2-for-2 stops beating 90-for-100.

**Bayesian average / beta-binomial updating.** Shrink small-sample estimates toward a prior: `BR = (C·m + Σx_i) / (C + n)` where `m` = tenant/global mean and `C` = prior strength in pseudo-observations ([Jules Jacobs, Bayesian scoring of ratings](https://julesjacobs.com/2015/08/17/bayesian-scoring-of-ratings.html), [Jeremy Kun, Bayesian ranking for rated items](https://www.jeremykun.com/2017/03/13/bayesian-ranking-for-rated-items/)). For binary outcomes the closed form is the Beta posterior mean: `p = (α + successes) / (α + β + n)` — a two-line deterministic "learn loop": pins increment successes, dismissals increment failures, the prior `Beta(α, β)` is per-tenant config, and the reason string writes itself ("3 pins, 1 dismiss on this rule; smoothed rate 0.57"). This is the natural math for turning eval rows into weight adjustments without any model training.

**Odoo's predictive lead scoring (naive Bayes on won/lost).** Odoo CRM computes win probability from per-variable won/lost frequency tables over the tenant's own pipeline history — variables like salesperson, source, language, stage, team, with the variable set **configurable per business** ([Odoo 18 docs: predictive lead scoring](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/crm/track_leads/lead_scoring.html), [Cybrosys explainer](https://www.cybrosys.com/blog/predictive-lead-scoring-and-probability-in-odoo-crm)). Naive Bayes here is just counting: each feature value contributes log-odds `ln( P(value|won) / P(value|lost) )` from the frequency tables — deterministic given the data, additive in log-space, and each term is a readable reason ("source=referral: wins 42% vs 18% baseline"). Mathematically it converges on the same object as credit-scorecard WOE (§1).

## 3. Open-source implementations worth pattern-borrowing

| Project | License | What it offers |
|---|---|---|
| [OptBinning](https://github.com/guillermo-navas-palencia/optbinning) | Apache 2.0 | Optimal/monotonic WOE binning, full scorecard modelling (PDO scaling), counterfactual explanations. The reference implementation for §1's scorecard math. Python. |
| [scorecardpy](https://github.com/ShichenXie/scorecardpy) | MIT (declared in [setup.py](https://github.com/ShichenXie/scorecardpy/blob/master/setup.py)) | Simpler WOE/IV/scorecard toolkit; good for lifting the WOE + points-scaling formulas without the optimizer. |
| [lifetimes](https://github.com/CamDavidsonPilon/lifetimes) | MIT | RFM summary utilities + BG/NBD / Pareto-NBD "probability alive" models over recency/frequency data; numpy/scipy/pandas only. The RFM data-prep code is directly liftable. |

| [json-rules-engine](https://github.com/CacheControl/json-rules-engine) | ISC | Rules as plain JSON (ALL/ANY nesting, operators, facts, priorities, no eval()); Node. Natural shape for per-tenant dealbreaker/fit rules stored as tenant config. |

**Reference-only (copyleft — patterns must be re-implemented, never embedded):**

- **Odoo CRM** — Community edition LGPLv3 ([license landscape](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/01/09/open-source-crm-benchmark-2026.html)); the naive-Bayes-from-won/lost-counts pattern and its "variables are per-business config" stance (§2) are the prize.
- **Mautic** — GPLv3 marketing automation with the most complete OSS **points system**: point triggers on events, point adjustments via form actions and campaign steps, manual adjustments, and **point groups** to categorize activity types ([Mautic points feature](https://mautic.org/features/lead-scoring-with-points/), [mautic.org](https://mautic.org/)). Its point-group taxonomy is a good template for signal families.
- **EspoCRM** — AGPLv3 since v8.1 ([license change announcement](https://www.espocrm.com/blog/espocrm-license/), [repo](https://github.com/espocrm/espocrm)). No native scoring; workflow/BPM patterns only.
- **SuiteCRM** — AGPLv3; deep enterprise feature set, no distinctive scoring math ([open-source CRM benchmark](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/01/09/open-source-crm-benchmark-2026.html)).
- **Twenty** — AGPL-3.0; core CRM objects/workflows, no scoring engine ([benchmark](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/01/09/open-source-crm-benchmark-2026.html)). (Consistent with the repo's existing Twenty=reference-only ruling.)

**Landscape note:** there is no mature, dedicated open-source *lead-scoring library*. The [GitHub lead-scoring topic](https://github.com/topics/lead-scoring) is dominated by one-off logistic-regression case-study notebooks. The real pattern sources are the adjacent traditions above: credit scorecards (deterministic, explainable, outcome-tuned) and RFM/CLV tooling.

## 4. Mapping onto the existing scorer

Existing components for reference: **REL** (embedding relevance vs ICP), **FIT** (structured matches), **COM** (completeness), **REC** (recency half-life); per-tenant weights normalized over armed signals; dealbreakers = hard zero; one reason per signal; append-only history; dismiss/pin eval rows.

| Item | Already have (under another name) | Genuinely new | Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Points-based (HubSpot/Marketo) | FIT + weights *is* additive points scoring | **Negative points** (penalties short of a hard zero); score decay on inactivity | Behavioral half needs event data (opens, visits) — **not collected yet** |
| Grading vs scoring (Pardot) | All four components exist | **Structural split into two published axes**: Fit grade (REL+FIT+COM) vs Engagement score (REC + future behavior) + quadrant routing; stops a hot-but-wrong lead from outranking a cold-but-perfect one | None for the fit axis; engagement axis waits on events |
| RFM | REC = the R | **F counter** (interaction count, log-compressed) and **M** (deal size when known); **quantile normalization vs the tenant's own lead pool** (self-calibrating cut-offs) | F needs a timestamped interaction log — **partial dependency**; quantile normalization works today on existing components |
| BANT/MEDDIC/CHAMP | FIT checklist is the same shape | **Named per-tenant checklist vocabulary** + conjunction gating ("no Authority contact ⇒ cap at nurture") — config/naming, near-zero math | Operator-entered qualification facts |
| Vendor predictive (Einstein/MadKudu) | One-reason-per-signal ≈ Einstein's top positives/negatives; MadKudu's fit×intent ≈ our components | Nothing embeddable (black-box or purchased-intent); **signal taxonomy** and the title→Job-Rank/Department canonicalization trick are reusable deterministic feature engineering | — |
| Credit scorecard WOE/PDO ≡ Odoo naive Bayes | Per-tenant weights (currently hand-set) | **Outcome-derived points**: per-bin `WOE = ln(%won/%lost)` from the tenant's own labeled history; IV to decide which signals deserve arming; PDO scaling to a human 0–1000 score; every bin = a reason string | Labeled outcomes — **exactly what dismiss/pin eval rows + won/lost already capture**; needs Laplace/Beta smoothing at small n |
| EdgeRank | w_e = our weights; d_e = REC | **Affinity multiplier** per lead↔tenant relationship (accumulated, decaying interaction credit); multiplicative composition option | Interaction history per lead — **event-log dependency** |
| PageRank | — | **Graph authority** over referral/same-company/shared-engagement edges | Relationship edges in schema — **largest dependency; defer** |
| HN gravity + penalties | REC ≈ decay | **Power-law decay option** (heavier tail than exponential) as per-tenant decay-shape config; **soft dealbreakers** = multiplicative penalties (×0.2) between full weight and hard zero | None |
| Reddit hot / log-compression | — | `log10(1+count)` on any frequency counter so repeats saturate | Counters (with F, above) |
| YouTube watch-time/velocity | Weight config can already prefer depth signals | **Surge flag**: trailing-window signal rate vs lifetime baseline ("heating up now") — an ordering/badge signal, not a quality component | Timestamped events |
| Wilson lower bound | — | **Small-sample honesty** for any rate-derived component or rule-performance stat; pure formula | Just counts n — none |
| Bayesian average / Beta posterior | Eval rows exist but no math consumes them | **The deterministic learn loop**: per-signal/per-rule `p = (α+pins)/(α+β+pins+dismissals)` with per-tenant prior; weight nudges proposed from posterior, applied via config (auditable), never silently | None — eval rows suffice |

## 5. Ranked recommendations (deterministic, reasons-first, small-team)

1. **Beta-posterior learn loop over eval rows (Bayesian average) + Wilson lower bound for any rate-derived stat.** Why: it is the missing consumer of the dismiss/pin eval rows — two closed-form formulas turn operator feedback into smoothed, per-tenant, fully auditable weight suggestions with self-writing reason strings, and Wilson keeps small samples honest. Prereqs: none — eval rows and counts already exist; prior (α, β) is per-tenant config. ([Jacobs](https://julesjacobs.com/2015/08/17/bayesian-scoring-of-ratings.html), [Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html))

2. **Two-axis Fit grade × Engagement score (Pardot grading-vs-scoring).** Why: the CRM industry's most battle-tested structural lesson — blending fit and interest into one number is the classic failure mode; splitting is a presentation/routing change over components already computed. Prereqs: none for the fit axis; engagement axis arms itself when behavioral events land. ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/pinpoint-hot-prospects-with-blended-pardot-scoring-and-grading/))

3. **WOE/scorecard points from won-lost history (credit-scorecard math ≡ Odoo's naive Bayes, done deterministically).** Why: the proven way to make weights *earned from the tenant's own outcomes* while staying additive and reason-per-bin explainable; IV also gives a principled "is this signal worth arming" test. Prereqs: a few dozen labeled outcomes per tenant; Beta/Laplace smoothing (from rec 1) until then; pattern-borrow from OptBinning (Apache 2.0) / scorecardpy (MIT), re-implement the Odoo pattern (LGPL, reference-only). ([OptBinning](https://github.com/guillermo-navas-palencia/optbinning), [Odoo docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/sales/crm/track_leads/lead_scoring.html))

4. **RFM frequency counter with log-compression + tenant-relative quantile scoring.** Why: adds the one classic dimension the scorer lacks (F), Reddit's `log10` keeps repeat signals from saturating, and quintile-vs-own-population scoring makes every component self-calibrating per tenant with a clean reason ("top 20% most-engaged leads this quarter"). Prereqs: a timestamped interaction log — **flag: behavioral events (email opens, site visits) are not collected yet**; quantile normalization of *existing* components needs nothing. ([Putler](https://www.putler.com/rfm-analysis/), [Reddit formula](https://medium.com/jp-tech/how-are-popular-ranking-algorithms-such-as-reddit-and-hacker-news-working-724e639ed9f7))

5. **HN-gravity "work this now" queue sort + soft-dealbreaker penalties.** Why: separates *quality* (the score) from *urgency* (score ÷ ageʰ) so the triage queue surfaces fresh-and-good first, and HN's multiplicative penalty tier gives operators a rung between full weight and hard zero. Prereqs: none — timestamps and the score exist; gravity exponent and penalty factors are per-tenant config. ([Salihefendic](https://medium.com/hacking-and-gonzo/how-hacker-news-ranking-algorithm-works-1d9b0cf2c08d), [Shirriff](http://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html))

Deferred with reasons: **PageRank graph authority** (highest ceiling, but needs relationship edges in the schema — revisit when referral/company graphs exist); **EdgeRank affinity multiplier** and **velocity surge flag** (both good, both blocked on the same behavioral event log as rec 4's F counter — adopt together when events arm); **vendor-style predictive ML** (excluded by the no-black-box constraint; keep only Einstein's title→rank/department canonicalization and MadKudu's fit×intent taxonomy as deterministic patterns).
