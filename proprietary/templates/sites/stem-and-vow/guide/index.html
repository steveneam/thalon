How this page was made

Stem & Vow is a fictional demonstration business — a portfolio piece showing what this content factory produces. No real florist, weddings, or suburb address is being advertised; the times, the order-book notes, and the place name are plausible fiction written to show how a real wedding florist's work would live on a page like this. All imagery is AI-generated.

Concept

A design survey of the wedding-florist genre found two defaults: the flower-shop e-commerce grid (burgundy and blush, "shop by occasion") and the wedding-romance template (posed couple hero, dream-wedding language, stat counters, petal-fall animations). Both were refused. The gap nobody fills: the florist as craft — the cold market run, the bench, and above all the timing, because a wedding florist's real skill is making flowers peak at 3pm Saturday.

So the page is a 72-hour countdown told through one bouquet. It opens at the payoff — the bouquet at the vow — then "72 hours earlier" runs the story back: market, bench, cool room, van, and returns to the ceremony with everything at peak. The same bouquet carries every frame. The scroll grammar is the focus pull: each chapter photograph racks from soft to sharp as it centres, the way a wedding film racks focus — and a warm halation quietly builds as you approach the vow, so the page gets dreamier exactly as the story does. The handwritten margin notes are the florist's own order book.

Design axes (assigned): cinematic-imagery primary — the photography carries the page and the code stays out of the way — with soft-organic secondary (the handwriting, the paper, the pacing). Type: Cormorant Garamond, Nunito Sans, Caveat — all OFL, served locally. Zero external requests; no analytics.

The honest details

  • Every human frame uses close-crop wedding-photography grammar — hands, a held bouquet, a figure dissolved in bokeh — so nobody stares at the camera and no face carries the page. That is both the professional register and a deliberate honesty choice for AI imagery.
  • The bloom triptych (tight → opening → peak) is the lay reading of the timing craft: one clock, told in petals rather than words.
  • The wink: one extra stem goes into every order, unlisted — it appears in the bench note, and the taller stem is findable in the ceremony photograph.
  • No packages, no urgency, no testimonials. The close asks for a date, because that is how this business would actually work.
  • Without JavaScript (or with reduced motion) every photograph is simply sharp and the story reads complete, top to bottom.

Method + passes

This page ran the full per-template design loop: a look-first genre survey, a scaffold with named asset slots in the design tool, an "alive" pass that proved the focus-pull and halation mechanics on placeholder frames, and only then the minted photography and this build. Iteration passes (fault-hunt + ambition lanes each) are logged here as they run:

  • Pass 1 — desktop walk on the live render: the rack-focus mechanic verified frame by frame (centred chapter sharp, neighbours 6–14px defocused, both scroll directions), the halation veil measured accruing 0 → 0.82 across the countdown; hero scrim contrast checked over the brightest dress areas; console clean; zero external requests.
  • Pass 2 — 390 px: no horizontal scroll at exactly 390; the nav collapses to the one Your-date link; hero, chapters, triptych and close all walked; the focus pull holds on the narrow layout.
  • Pass 3 — honesty + access sweep: keyboard walk (skip link, nav, the date CTA, the guide link — focus ring on all); without JavaScript every photo is simply sharp and the story reads complete (the blur only ever comes FROM JavaScript, so the page degrades to the finished sharp essay); reduced-motion holds a gentle fixed veil; every alt describes its actual frame, including the taller +1 stem in the ceremony image. Squint test against the other fifteen portfolio sites: nothing else is a photographic countdown. Ceiling call: the focus pull + halation IS the page's one loud mechanism; the photography does the rest on purpose.

Provenance

Every image was minted on the paid tier and pinned at mint time into content-addressed storage with a full provenance manifest (model, prompt, parameters, credits, license tier, job id). The files this page serves are deterministic derivations (resize, re-encode) of those pinned originals.
