How this page was made

Vance & Alder is a fictional demonstration business. Every image on the landing page is AI-generated, minted specifically for this design, and recorded with full provenance (model, prompt, spend, license tier) at generation time.

The idea

The page is built as a legal document rather than a website about lawyers: a ruled masthead, a casebook index with leader dots, a two-column argument with real footnotes, and a matter ledger whose client names are redacted — bars that swipe in, refuse on hover, and never lift, because a firm that shows you its clients’ names would show someone yours. A sticky running head carries the file number and the section you are reading past, with a thin oxblood reading-progress rule; the principals’ signatures ink themselves left to right as you reach them.

Method

  • Look first. Studied law-firm landing pages and editorial/print design on Dribbble and Pinterest (look-and-learn only — no reference imagery is ever downloaded into the pipeline). The genre default — suited-team photography, navy and gold, “Trusted Legal Solutions,” stat badges, scales-of-justice statues — appeared more than fifteen times and was deliberately avoided in full.
  • Design scaffold. The page was scaffolded and then graduated to a final motion design in a working design surface before any production code, with named slots for every image.
  • Minted imagery. The hero — a brass nameplate that must legibly read VANCE & ALDER, SOLICITORS — was generated with Nano Banana Pro, a text-precise image model, and read perfectly on the first take. Three scene plates were generated with Soul v2 (Higgsfield). One Soul v2 take was rejected with reasons on record: it printed garbled pseudo-lettering across the file spines at the page’s focal point and added a scales-of-justice statue from the avoided-cliché list; the retake composed every printable surface out of frame. Total image spend for the page: about 2.5 credits.
  • Code, not credits. The running head, progress rule, redaction behaviour, develop-in plates and signature reveals are plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript in one self-contained file — no frameworks, no external hosts, fonts vendored under their SIL Open Font License (Newsreader and Courier Prime).

Accessibility & honesty

  • Reduced-motion users get the complete page with every bar and signature already applied; nothing animates.
  • Without JavaScript the full page reads statically, redactions included.
  • All copy is illustrative; no real firm, matters, clients or outcomes are described, and no unverifiable claims are made.

Iteration passes

  • Pass 1 — fault-hunt: the design surface’s utility gap classes silently failed, running the masthead navigation into one string and letting the counsel columns touch; replaced with explicit gaps. Ambition: committed to the sticky running head with a reading-progress rule instead of a conventional navigation bar.
  • Pass 2 — fault-hunt: 390 px overflow from three sources (the navigation row, the 76 px hero line, the mono ledger rows) fixed with clamps and wraps. Ambition: the matter ledger inverted to ink with paper-cream redaction bars that refuse on hover.
  • Pass 3 — fault-hunt: without JavaScript the entrance states would have hidden content forever; motion-hidden states are now gated behind a js class so the no-script page is complete. Ambition: plates develop in like photographs in a tray, and the principals’ signatures ink themselves.
