How this page was made

Northpace is a fictional demonstration business. No real studio, no real coaches, no real harbor loop — the plan prices are sample prices and the intro-run schedule is invented. This site is one entry in a portfolio of design templates, each built for a different kind of business.

The design idea

A coached runner's world is the pre-dawn: streetlights, cold air, a plan for the morning. Most running sites shout — dark gyms, neon, motion blur. This one stays calm and cold instead: a polar-night page under a living aurora (drawn in code, drifting slowly, leaning gently toward your pointer), that runs the whole scroll and only surrenders at the end, where the page grades into a pale dawn over the final viewport — the payoff every early alarm is buying.

The page has one tempo: 180 steps per minute, the cadence the studio coaches first. It lives in small instruments — a metronome bar, a pulsing beat dot, liquid rising inside the big numerals — rather than in page-wide motion; ambient movement stays slow and tidal so it never fights the sky. The centerpiece is the session: scrolling runs a real coached workout to scale, and the display type embodies the effort — threshold reps set heavy and condensed, floats light and wide — while a profile chart draws the work as you do it.

Type and materials

  • Anybody (variable, weight 100–900 × width 50–150%): the display voice. The variable axes are load-bearing — the workout phases speak through weight and width, and the wordmark letters swell under the pointer like a wave through the word.
  • Spline Sans Mono (variable): timecodes, watch-face chips, splits, captions — the sports-watch voice.
  • Both typefaces are open-source under the SIL Open Font License and served from this site; their license texts ship in the site's fonts/ directory.
  • No frameworks, no third-party code, no trackers, and no network calls from any script: what happens on this page stays on this page.

Imagery

Every photograph on the page is AI-generated, minted for this site on a paid, commercially-licensed plan. Four cinematic stills were minted at build, prompted as one cold morning (aurora road, streetlight miles, trail day, first-light track). In a later casting audit the trail-day frame was retired: its subject read as standing rather than running, the "headlamp" beam came from somewhere off-frame, and the frame crushed to near-black at phone size. Its replacement was minted for exactly that slot — an everyday runner at first light, side-on and absorbed in the run. The first take of the replacement was rejected because the runner looked into the camera; the keeper doesn't. Full provenance for every image (model, prompt, spend, license tier — rejected takes included) is recorded in the studio's asset register at mint time. The aurora itself is not an image — it is drawn live on a canvas, so it can drift, keep the cadence, and answer the pointer.

Build notes: iteration passes

The page went through repeated full passes, each hunting faults (layout, contrast, motion honesty, accessibility) and pushing one deliberate ambition item.

  1. PASS 1 — Fault lane: the hero kicker inherited the prose measure and wrapped; the 180 numerals' liquid fill was clipped against the wrong box (it included the unit line below, so the fill rendered as a slab under the digits — re-anchored to its own wrapper); the still cards kept their HTML height attribute while their width shrank, distorting the frames (global height:auto); the dawn's pale stops landed inside the closing photograph, leaving "See you at 05:00" dark-on-dark (gradient re-mapped, gap enlarged); the closing caption was invisible over the dark track (lightened); a missing favicon 404'd. Ambition lane: the sky answers the pointer — aurora ribbons lean toward the cursor and bloom softly where it rests.
  2. PASS 2 — Fault lane: the phone nav overflowed (collapsed to the wordmark + Guide below 640px); the sticky session scene could clip its own readout on short viewports (it now enhances only when the whole readout fits; otherwise the complete static plan is shown); the static fallback text promised "scroll to run it" even where nothing would happen (made honest); live watch chips showed frozen zeros outside the live scene (hidden when static). Ambition lane: the wordmark is material — letters swell in weight under the pointer.
  3. PASS 3 — Fault lane: a computed contrast sweep failed the quietest text tier and the dawn schedule note (both re-inked to clear AA on their actual backgrounds; hairline dashes separated from text color); without JavaScript the entrance choreography left the whole page invisible — the hidden initial state is now gated behind a JS-present class, so the no-script page is complete and static; keyboard walk verified (skip link, logical order, visible focus on the night field and on the dawn); reduced-motion verified honest (static sky, full content, no pulse, no sticky scene). Mid-build direction from review: the headers' springy scroll-width effect and the wordmark's per-step shimmer read as jitter against the calm sky — idle motion re-tuned to a slow tide, tempo kept only in the contained instruments, big type now moves only on interaction or state change. Ambition lane: ceiling call — the living sky, the cadence instruments, the session scene and the dawn arrival are this page's memorable moments; anything further would be noise against the calm.
  4. PASS 4 — a later taste pass. Fault lane: the session's numbers didn't add up — the plan lists 10:00 + 4 × 6:00 + 3 × 2:00 + 10:00 but the note said "48:00 total" (it's 50:00), and the effort chart's x-axis wasn't drawn to time scale, so the moving cursor crossed each plateau out of step with the highlighted plan row (chart redrawn time-linear, one minute = twelve pixels; total corrected); the aurora-road alt text promised "a tiny runner far away" that no zoom of the frame actually shows (alt made honest); the wordmark's pointer-swell was measured before the variable font loaded, so the swell could sit off-center until a resize (re-measured when fonts arrive). Ambition lane: the coaching prose now shows its data — each method card carries a code-drawn instrument in place of a paragraph: a cadence rail (today 164, coached toward 180) with the honest-pace readout, the sample training week with each session's why, and a fortnight of logged runs where the missed day stays on the chart as data and the next week visibly bends −10%.
  5. PASS 5 — Fault lane: a casting audit over every human frame — the trail-day still failed (standing posture, off-frame light source, illegible at phone scale) and was replaced by the first-light runner (see Imagery above); the sample week first drawn for the build card showed one quality session and one drill day against the programs' "two coached quality sessions" and "drills, twice weekly" promises — the week was recut until every countable claim matched the copy; contrast of every new instrument tone was re-computed against its actual card background (all clear AA); the new instruments are static HTML and CSS — complete without JavaScript, untouched by reduced motion. Ambition lane: each program card now draws the block it sells — twelve weeks of volume with the cutbacks visible, the quality share capped in green on Threshold, the taper and a race-day star on Peak — and the page allows itself exactly one wink: reach the dawn and "See you at 05:00." flickers, once, to 05:09. The snooze that loses.

Honesty rules this page follows

  • No invented testimonials, race results, awards or member counts.
  • No fake urgency or scarcity — the only countdown is a fictional workout clock that says what it is.
  • Plan prices are labeled sample pricing; the booking address uses a reserved demonstration domain.
  • The fictional nature of the business is disclosed (this page and the site footer).
