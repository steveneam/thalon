Coffee is three things before it is a drink. We only make the first one — the other two happen in your kitchen, in about four minutes, and that is where most of it is won or lost.

One — the bean

It starts as fruit.

Every bean in that cup was the seed of a small red fruit, picked by hand on a hillside high enough that the nights stay cold and the cherry takes its time about ripening. We buy from three farms and we name all three. Nothing here is a blend of whatever was cheap that month.

The roast

We roast in twelve-kilo batches on the Tuesday before we ship, and we stop earlier than most: far enough in to lose the grassiness, not so far that everything on the table tastes of the roaster instead of the farm. What arrives is brown, not black.

Two — the grind

Then we take it apart.

Whole, a roasted bean keeps nearly everything worth tasting locked in oil behind a hard shell, and it will hold onto it for weeks on a shelf.

Ground, the same coffee has hours. Breaking it up multiplies its surface area by something close to a thousand, and every volatile thing in it starts leaving immediately — which is most of what you were going to taste.

Three — the part that is yours

This is the bit we can't do for you.

So here is the only thing we would actually ask you to change, and it costs nothing: grind it when you are about to drink it, rather than when you buy it.

A week-old bag of whole beans beats a day-old bag of pre-ground, and it is not close. That holds whatever you brew it in — which is why we don't sell it ready ground. Not as a principle. We would just be selling you the worse half of our own work.

Four — the cup

Then, water.

Hot water goes in and everything the hillside put there comes back out: first as a bloom lifting off the bed, then as colour, then as the only part of any of this that was ever the point.

We are not going to tell you a ratio. There is one printed on the bag. It is a starting place, and it is wrong for somebody.

The name

The first hour.

That is what Morningside is named for. Not a place — a time of day. It is when we drink it, it is what we roast towards, and it is the only claim on this page we would defend without a single qualification.

We have argued about the grind, in this building, every week, since we opened. Every batch gets re-ground and re-tasted and it has settled nothing at all. If you work it out, please tell us.
