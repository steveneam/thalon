Method note

How this page was made

Small Hours is not a real business. It is a demonstration page — a fictional botanical perfumery invented to show how a landing page can be built around a working instrument rather than around a photograph of a bottle. Nothing on it is for sale, there is no bench and there are no Thursdays.

Every photograph and the moving sequence on this page are AI-generated. None of them is a photograph of a real place, a real person or a real plant. They were generated to order for this page, and the models and prompts are listed below.

What is real

The chemistry is. The compounds named in the instrument are genuine constituents of jasmine flower scent, and the broad shape the instrument shows — leaf-green volatiles dominating a closed bud, benzyl acetate and linalool arriving as the corolla opens, jasmone and indole peaking in the small hours, total emission falling once the sun is on the row — is the real behaviour of the real plant. Headspace analysis is a real technique: a bell over a living, uncut flower, the air drawn off and read.

But the exact numbers are illustrative. They are plausible and internally consistent — each hour's shares sum to 100, and the page's own test suite asserts that the figures printed in the markup are the same figures the script reads — but no gas chromatograph was run for this page, and "bell 040" is a fiction. Treat the instrument as an honest diagram of a real phenomenon, not as data.

Indole is the one detail worth keeping: it genuinely smells of jasmine at a whisper and of decay at a shout, and it genuinely is a large part of why jasmine is difficult. That is not a flourish invented for the page.

The instrument

The stage is one clock. Scrolling the four chapters moves the night from 22:00 to 05:20, and that single clock drives the scrubbed bloom, all seven compound bars, the stacked colour band, the four readouts and the chapter marker. There is no second animation system anywhere on the page.

The clock follows the prose, not the scrollbar. Each chapter carries its own hour as a data-hour attribute and the readout shows the anchor the reader is actually on — so the instrument can never be reporting 04:00 while the words beside it are still describing one in the morning.

The moving image is not a video element. Seeking a compressed video by setting currentTime lands on keyframes and janks, so the generated take was decoded once into 36 stills and the page swaps between those under scroll. The mp4 stays the pinned original; the frames are the derive. A frame that has not finished decoding paints nothing, so the swap walks back to the nearest decoded neighbour rather than letting the stage go blank.

Without JavaScript

The page is built static-first and this claim is tested, not asserted: the compound table, all four chapters, every readout, the wink and the fully-open frame are real markup in the document. With scripting off you get the whole page and a still flower instead of a moving one. With prefers-reduced-motion set, the same — nothing is animated and nothing is withheld.

The bloom was generated between two of our own still mints — a closed bud as the start frame and the fully-open flower as the end frame — so the sequence interpolates between two images we made rather than being invented whole. Both of those keyframes, and four discarded candidates, were also minted here; the session's total spend was 35.00 credits, of which 17.50 was the moving sequence and 17.50 was eleven still generations including the rejects. Individual image prices are marked with a tilde because the batch price is exact and the per-image split is not.

Every asset was pinned at mint time with its full provenance — model, prompt, parameters, credits, licence tier and job id — before it reached this page. No image on this page is a stock photograph, and no vendor URL is served from it; everything is a local file.

Two things the build got wrong first

The instrument was invisible. Drawn first as vertical bars with rotated labels, the bars resolved to zero height and the most important element on the page rendered as nothing — while the source read perfectly well. It was rebuilt as horizontal rows, which is both legible and the shape the discipline's own documents have always had: a name on the left, a quantity on the right.

The flower had the wrong number of petals. The first anchor image came back with eight, where Jasminum grandiflorum has five. Since the whole page names one species, it was re-generated with the count stated explicitly rather than left to be noticed by anyone who grows the plant. The camera position of every frame was then measured rather than eyeballed — the bud and the open flower sit within about a pixel of each other, which is why the sequence does not swim as you scroll it.
