### Why the palette axis is load-bearing here and not applied

The page's colour world **is the instrument's output**. Every tracked compound
owns a colour; the stacked "reading" bar at the top of the instrument is the
whole emission of that moment expressed as one band of colour; and because the
emission changes as the flower opens, **the page's colour changes on the same
scroll clock**. Nothing is themed. This is Piesse's 1857 odophone — the
historic attempt to map scent onto a scale — done honestly, which is the
cheapest possible way to earn the axis instead of decorating with it.

- Palette seed: **night** (a blue-black, never pure black), **lamp** (the warm
  sodium-amber a picker carries), and **paper** (a warm unbleached off-white
  for the daylight half of the page). Against those three grounds sit the
  **seven compound colours**, which are the only saturated colour on the page
  and are never used decoratively — a swatch on this page always means a
  molecule. The page runs **dark at the instrument and light everywhere else**,
  because the instrument is the only part that happens at night.
- Type: **Alegreya** (roman + italic — the italic carries the Latin binomials,
  and Alegreya's calligraphic warmth is the opposite of the fashion-luxury
  high-contrast serif the vertical defaults to) + **Azeret Mono** (every piece
  of instrument apparatus: compound names, values, readouts, hour stamps).
  **Two families**, honest to a discipline that has exactly two documents — the
  ledger and the readout. Both OFL, both vendored, **both portfolio-new**
  (verified against the ~40 faces already vendored across the other 21 sites;
  Fraunces, Newsreader, Instrument Serif, Spectral, Martian/Plex/Space Mono are
  all already taken).
- Motion budget: **one scroll clock — the night, 22:00 → 05:20.** One clock
  drives the scrubbed bloom, all seven compound bars, the stacked reading, four
  readouts and the chapter marker. **No second animation system.** Reduced
  motion and no-JS both get the fully-open keyframe as a static still, with
  every chapter, the full compound table and the wink still in the markup.

## Look-first taste notes (Dribbble · Pinterest · motionsites.ai)

1. **The category default is one image, and it is astonishingly uniform: the
   bottle on a plinth, in the dark.** Dribbble's "perfume website" returns page
   after page of near-black grounds, gold and amber, script-italic display type
   and the same four sentences — *Elevate Your Senses · Unveil Your Signature
   Scent · Timeless Scents, Lasting Impressions · Wrap Yourself in Noir
   Elegance*. Every one of them sells **the finished bottle as a luxury
   object**. **Refused by name.** This page sells **the living flower and the
   forty minutes of its air**; the bottles appear once, near the end, in
   daylight, as the thing you can actually buy.
2. **The adjacent default is the consumer fragrance journal** — TOP / HEART /
   BASE printables, "performance & rating", "season & occasion". This is real
   and it is everywhere, but it is the **marketing** artifact, not the bench
   document. It fails the schematic test in the subtle way (§below). **Refused.**
3. **The real artefact, from Pinterest: the perfumer's ledger.** A 19th-century
   *Houbigant, Marchand Parfumeur* sheet — iron-gall handwriting on cream laid
   paper under an engraved botanical vignette — and beside it typeset apothecary
   formulae with **dotted leaders running out to the quantity**. **The principle
   to steal: a formula is a LIST OF MATERIALS AND HOW MUCH**, set as a table
   with the name flush left and the number flush right. That is exactly the
   shape the instrument takes, so the instrument inherits four centuries of the
   discipline's own typography instead of inventing a chart.
4. **motionsites.ai, read double-edged as the meta-prompt instructs.** Its own
   gallery is the AI-landing default it warns about — near-black ground,
   glowing gradient headline (*"UNLOCK YOUR AI DESIGN SUPERPOWERS"*), neon 3D.
   **Note the collision:** the perfume default and the AI default are *the same
   page* — dark ground, glow, gold. A dark perfume site would land on both at
   once. The refusal that costs nothing: **the page's brightest moment is its
   first**, and the dark is rationed to the one chapter that literally happens
   at night.
5. **The one card worth stealing from, also motionsites:** a pale, high-key page
   built around a single organic specimen dead centre (*"Learn to see
   brilliantly"*). **Principle: one specimen, enormous, at high key, with the
   whole page arranged around it** — which is the stage this site already wants.

## The schematic test, applied before drawing anything

The banked idea for site C was a **bloom video**, which is a mint rather than a
drawing, so it dodges the picture-of-a-thing trap that cost ⑳ three rounds. The
spec says so explicitly and says the equivalent question is **what a perfumer's
own document looks like**. Applying it:

- **The note pyramid (top / heart / base): FAILS.** It is real in the industry
  and it is on every fragrance page on earth — but it is the **sales sheet**,
  not the bench document, and it is static by construction. Drawing it would be
  drawing the marketing. **Not built.**
- **The formula (materials × parts): PASSES, but it does not move.** It is the
  right *shape* — name left, quantity right — and it is stolen for the
  instrument's typography (taste note 3). But a formula is a fixed recipe; it
  has no clock, so it cannot be the scroll instrument.
- **The drydown curve: PASSES, wrong subject.** Real, and it moves — but it is
  about a finished perfume on skin, which would make the page about the bottle
  again.
- **HEADSPACE ANALYSIS: PASSES, and it is the only candidate on the same clock
  as the video.** ✅ **BUILT.** A glass bell over a *living, uncut* flower, air
  drawn off for forty minutes, read as a list of volatile compounds and their
  concentrations at a timestamp. It is a genuine and specifically *botanical*
  perfumery technique (the discipline's whole modern claim to capturing flowers
  too fragile to distil), it produces exactly the ledger-shaped document taste
  note 3 found, and its output **changes hour by hour while the flower opens**.

**Why this is the structural answer and not merely a clever one.** If the
instrument were a formula or a pyramid, the mandated bloom video would sit
*beside* it as decoration — a pretty thing next to a list. With headspace, the
video **is the specimen under the bell and the instrument is what the bell
reads**. They are one clock and neither survives without the other. That is the
same move that made ㉑ work, in a genuinely different grammar — and it gets the
founder's ㉑ ranking critique right from the first line rather than by a
rebalance afterwards: **the photograph leads and the instrument reads off it.**

## The mock's findings (claude-design stage, run first)

The mock earned its keep for the third site running. Three faults, all found by
rendering it and none visible in the source:

1. **The instrument was invisible.** Built first as vertical bars, the bars
   resolved to zero height (percentage heights against an auto-height track) —
   **the single most important element on the page rendered as nothing**, and
   the source read fine. Refactored to **horizontal rows**, which is also the
   correct form for the discipline (taste note 3) and the only form that holds
   seven long chemical names legibly. The vertical form had put 9px rotated
   labels under the bars: at that size it is decoration, not information —
   the exact defect ㉑'s annotation type had at 4px.
2. **The clock contradicted the prose in the mock's own placeholder values** —
   chapter reading `01:00 · It begins` beside a readout reading `03:40 · OPEN`.
   Harmless in a mock, and it is precisely the ㉑ s105 failure the meta-prompt
   now warns about (*THE CLOCK FOLLOWS THE PROSE, NOT THE SCROLLBAR*). **Build
   requirement, and it is a test, not an intention:** each chapter carries its
   own `data-hour`, the clock interpolates between those anchors, and the
   rendered pair must be *measured* in agreement.
3. **Two different quantities were both wearing a `%` sign.** The instrument
   reports indole as a share of *what the flower emits* (~6% at peak); the
   drama chapter talks about indole as a dose *in a finished blend* (0.1% vs
   5%). A careful reader would have read the second as contradicting the first.
   **Fixed in the copy by labelling both** — `INDOLE, OF EMISSION` on the
   readout and `0.1% IN THE BLEND` on the scale, plus one sentence naming the
   distinction. This is §casting (3)-extended-to-DATA doing its job at pre-plan
   cost instead of at fix-round cost.

## The instrument

**Jasminum grandiflorum · one flower · one night · bell 040.**

Pinned stage, the photograph leading at ~61% of the stage area (measured in the
mock; ㉑ shipped 63% after its rebalance and that is the target). Under it:

- **The reading** — one stacked horizontal band, the seven compounds' shares as
  seven colour segments plus an "everything else" remainder. This is the whole
  smell of that moment as a single strip of colour, and it is where the palette
  axis becomes the instrument rather than the theme.
- **The profile** — seven rows, compound name flush left in mono, coloured bar,
  value flush right, in the ledger's own typography. Plus the honest remainder
  row: a real chromatogram has a long tail, and hiding it would make the seven
  sum to 100 and lie.
- **Four readouts** — `HOUR` · `TOTAL EMISSION` · `INDOLE, OF EMISSION` ·
  `STATE`.

**The chapters are the score and the clock follows them** (㉑ s105 ratchet):

| Chapter | `data-hour` | Bloom state | What the profile does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tight, and green | `22:00` | keyframe 1 — closed bud | almost nothing but leaf volatiles; the green row is most of a very short bar |
| It begins | `01:00` | keyframe 2 — half open | benzyl acetate arrives and dominates; linalool under it |
| Fully open. The animal arrives | `03:40` | keyframe 3 — fully open | jasmone and indole peak; total emission at maximum |
| First light, and we pick | `05:20` | keyframe 3, held | total emission already falling — heat takes the light molecules first |

**Static-first is an honesty gate** (㉑ s105): the schedule lives in ONE inert
`<script type="application/json">` block, the static markup is generated from
it, and the runtime reads the same block. Nothing can drift, and `/guide` can
say the page works without JavaScript **because it does**.

**The four silent killers get measured, not assumed**: stage column stretches
(grid, not `align-items:flex-start`), **no `overflow` on any ancestor**, the
narrow layout moves `position:sticky` onto the column, and the reading line is
measured from the stage's bottom edge on narrow screens.

## Casting & social register

Botanical perfumery is a **craft/product** purchase, not a social one, so the
social-register dial reads **LOW-to-MIDDLE**: nature and the object lead, one
absorbed practitioner is the trust signal, no groups, no camera-staring.
Per §casting (7) the vertical is craft rather than professional-serious, and
per §9 the **object is the protagonist and nature is the supporting cast** —
which here is unusually literal, because the object *is* a plant.

- **Lead figure: one picker, hands and forearms, at a lamp, absorbed.** Faces
  present only in the field-at-dawn hero and never zoomed, never to camera.
- **Printable-surface risk is LOW** (no machinery, no workwear in close-up, no
  labels) — but the **bottles in §05 are the exception and they are the highest
  text risk on the page**: a bottle is a flat printable surface and soul-class
  will letter it unprompted. **Compose out, never negate** — the bottles are
  shot with **no label at all** (plain glass, wax over the stopper), described
  positively as "unlabelled apothecary glass", and if lettering appears anyway
  the slot routes to the text-precise seat or the crop composes the face out.
- **Count-anchoring:** §05 promises "three absolutes and one extrait" — so any
  frame containing bottles is prompted for **fewer** bottles than the copy
  names, per §casting (5).
- **Countable claims** (§casting (3), extended to DATA at s105): the page names
  a species, an hour and seven compounds. Every generated image must agree with
  all four — a jasmine that is a rose, or an open flower under the 22:00
  chapter, is the ㉑ hydrangea failure repeated. **Checked at the fix round
  against the instrument's own JSON, not against memory.**

## What the BUILD found (added after the fix round — the mock could not see these)

Five defects, and **every one of them was invisible until the page was rendered
and scrolled**; none was caught by types, lint or the 3,452-test suite.

1. **THE SCRUB WAS DEAD, AND THE PAGE LOOKED PERFECT.** The static markup ships
   one frame carrying `.on` so the no-JS stage is not blank; the runtime's
   `shown` index started at `-1`, so the first swap had nothing to clear, that
   frame stayed lit, and — the frames being absolutely stacked — it kept
   painting over every frame the scroll chose. **The flower never moved.** Every
   still screenshot was correct, the clock and readouts moved correctly, the
   console was silent. Found only by counting lit frames across a scroll sweep
   (two were lit, 32 and 35). This is the s106 lesson recurring one level
   deeper: it was not enough to render it, and not even enough to *look* at it
   moving — it had to be **counted**. Ratcheted in `template-portfolio.test.ts`,
   and the ratchet was proven to fail against this exact defect before it was
   trusted.
2. **The clock contradicted the prose — the very thing this page's code comments
   claim it prevents.** Measuring the scroll fraction top-of-chapter to
   top-of-chapter meant a chapter squarely on the reading line already sat half
   way to the next anchor, so the readout showed **01:00 beside "the bud is
   shut"** and **05:20 beside "fully open"**. Fixed by anchoring on chapter
   *centres*, so "centred on a chapter" means exactly that hour's values, and by
   making the hour and state name the active chapter outright rather than a
   rounded interpolation.
3. **`margin: 0 auto` on a GRID ITEM shrink-wraps it.** The hero's top bar
   collapsed to 418px in a 1440px viewport while every other `.wrap` was fine,
   because only that one was itself a grid item. `width: 100%` on `.wrap`.
4. **The mobile clock lagged a whole chapter**, then the highlight and the
   nearest chapter disagreed for the second half of every gap. Two fixes: the
   reading line is the band *below* the sheet, and the active chapter is the
   **nearest** anchor (`Math.round(k)`) rather than the last one whose centre
   was passed.
5. **Shrinking the mobile sheet does not buy reading room** — the instrument has
   a hard minimum height, so every pixel comes off the *photograph* (measured:
   62svh → 54svh dropped it from 32% of the stage to 22%). The band was fixed by
   anchoring the clock on the **heading** instead, and the sheet stayed tall.

**Carried, recorded not fixed:** the mobile stage's vertical rhythm is airier
than it should be — a chapter's heading clears the sheet on some scroll
positions and not others, so the reader occasionally meets a paragraph before
its title. The correctness invariants all hold there (hour always equals the
highlighted chapter, exactly one lit frame, monotonic bloom, no horizontal
scroll); this is ergonomics, and it is the first thing to spend a fix round on.
