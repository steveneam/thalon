How this page was made

First Crack is a fictional demonstration business — a portfolio piece showing what this content factory produces. No real roastery, café, or coffee is being sold. All imagery is AI-generated. Honest note: the batch numbers, roast curves, cupping scores, and address are plausible fiction written to show how a real roaster's data would live on a page like this.

Concept

Coffee roasters keep the most beautiful instrument in food — the roast log — and no roastery website shows it. Design survey of the genre found the same default everywhere: kraft-bag product grids, brown-on-brown, macro bean photos (even the industry's most data-driven roaster hides its data behind a shop grid). This page refuses that default: the page IS a roast log. Scrolling replays batch 447 from charge to drop on a real-shaped curve; first crack at 8:52 is the page's one loud moment — the wordmark's C cracks and stays cracked. The palette is the roast's own gradient (green bean → gold → amber → roast brown, ember reserved for first crack), not café black.

Design axes (assigned): data-instrument primary — the warm-craft register: graph paper, stamped micro-caps, printed-log tables — with cinematic imagery secondary. Type: Young Serif · Hanken Grotesk · Space Mono, all OFL, served locally. Zero external requests; no analytics.

The honest instrument

  • The curve's shape is a real roast profile (charge drop to turning point, Maillard flattening, first-crack inflection, development window) — the numbers are fictional but the physics is not.
  • Without JavaScript (or with reduced motion) you get the finished, fully annotated log — the same truth, already told.
  • The batch log includes batch 446, which ran long and became staff espresso. A roaster who publishes the log publishes the misses; a demo about honest instruments has to model that too.
  • No fake urgency, no invented testimonials, no awards. Cupping bars top out well below 10 on purpose.

Method + passes

Built as one self-contained HTML file: the curve is hand-drawn SVG, the readout and scroll replay are ~90 lines of dependency-free JavaScript, the cupping bars and log table are plain markup. Iteration passes (fault-hunt + ambition lanes each) are logged here as they run:

  • Pass 1 — pre-render fixes: a broken CSS line removed, missing .sr-only defined, the chart given its own horizontal scroll on phones so axis labels stay legible (the page never scrolls sideways), unused italic font dropped, all three image alts rewritten to describe the actual frames.
  • Pass 2 — browser pass, both lanes. Fault-hunt: a flex min-size bug let the chart's minimum width push the whole page into horizontal scroll at 390px (fixed at the container, verified 390 exactly); the ember accent measured ~3.6:1 as text on cream and failed AA — a darker ember-ink now carries every ember word while the vivid ember keeps the lines, band, and crack; two chart labels un-crowded. Ambition: the log's DEV column became a small instrument — each batch's development ratio as a bar, with 446's overshoot in ember so the confession row reads at a glance.
  • Pass 3 — final sweep: keyboard walk (skip link, focus rings, no interactive element unlabeled), the chart's accessible title/description verified as the no-JS/AT twin of the replay, hero-to-log tonal cut softened, squint test against the other thirteen portfolio sites — nothing else is a graph-paper roast log. Ceiling call: the replay + crack is the page's one loud moment by design; nothing further added.
  • Founder fix round (same day, three notes): (1) the workshop photo rendered stretched — the global img rule lacked height:auto; fixed here and the portfolio's automated stretch check was tightened so a non-img rule can never satisfy it again. (2) Each coffee card now opens with its brewed result — filter in glass, milk pour, cold brew — so you can see the colour and texture the beans become (one cold-brew take rejected: the model stood the glass ON the ice cubes). (3) The roast section became a split instrument: the curve on the left, and on the right the bean itself — five minted macro states (green → drying → Maillard → first crack → dropped) crossfading on the same scroll clock as the graph, with a stage chip. Without JavaScript the five states render as a labelled filmstrip. One new layout catch fixed in the round: at phone width the panel's aspect-ratio derived its width from a min-height and pushed the page sideways — width now leads.

What the review taught (kept for every future build)

Two of the founder's fix-round notes were bigger than this page, and both are now standing rules in the factory's build method:

  • Show the consumable result. Beans sell to roasters; cups sell to drinkers. Any page for something tasted, worn, or lived in must show what the customer actually receives — colour, texture, the imagined smell — beside the craft that makes it.
  • Every expert instrument gets a lay twin. A roast curve rewards the few who can read it; the bean itself speaks to everyone. When a page's signature element is a data instrument, it is paired with a sensory representation of the same information on the same clock — one scroll, two readings.
