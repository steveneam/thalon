How this page was made

Wagtail & Co is a fictional demonstration business. The dogs, the day journal, the rates and the suburbs list are illustrative copy for a demo — no real service exists. All imagery is AI-generated. Banjo and Pip, regrettably, are not real dogs.

Design

A look-first study of pet-care sites and hand-drawn organic web design came first. The pet genre default — rounded SaaS cards, cartoon mascots, bright blue cheer — was set aside. The page instead reads as a dog's day journal: warm photography carries the page while the hand-drawn register lives in the margins (a leash line that draws itself down the day as you scroll, handwritten annotations, an underline that behaves like a crayon). Soft pacing on cream paper; nothing thunks, everything eases.

Type: Baloo 2, Karla and Gochi Hand — all SIL Open Font License, vendored locally with their licenses. Single self-contained file, no frameworks, no external requests, reduced-motion and no-JS complete.

Imagery

Three photographs were generated through the Higgsfield API on a paid license tier; each original is pinned in a content-addressed store with full provenance (model, prompt, credits, license tier). The models named are the models that actually ran.

Honesty note: the hero prompt asked for no leash; a faint lead strap is visible low in the frame's shadow. No real signage, brands or people appear anywhere.

The walk image took four takes, honestly logged: the original showed five dogs against this page's own "four dogs per walker, never more" rule (caught at founder review), and two retakes produced six dogs on the road and a printed t-shirt. The keeper shows a walker with three dogs on the footpath — under the cap, as it should be. Both journal photographs were also regenerated at the higher 2k tier and displayed larger after review feedback that they sat too small on the page.

Iteration passes

  • Pass 1 (fault-hunt): journal reveal + leash-line draw verified live in the browser (entries ease in as they arrive, the line draws with scroll progress); all three images confirmed loading; hero annotation position checked against the blob mask.
  • Pass 2 (ambition-push + structure): nap polaroid moved beside its text at desktop widths to fix the journal's rhythm; breathing blob mask on the hero; crayon underline; live-easing entries; dead CSS pruned.
  • Pass 3 (fault-hunt): 390 px pass (single column journal, leash hidden, no horizontal overflow), reduced-motion audit (blob static, entries visible, line pre-drawn), no-JS audit (all content visible without any script).
  • Pass 4 (founder review round): the walk photograph was replaced — a woman walker for calm, safe energy, exactly three dogs (under the pack cap), on the footpath — and the nap regenerated at 2k; both journal images now display roughly 40% larger after feedback that they sat too compressed. Paw prints were sprinkled around section corners for playfulness (decorative, hidden from screen readers). Re-verified: 390 px (a 2px overflow from the enlarged tilted polaroid's corners was clipped at its container), desktop walk, images loading, console clean.
  • Pass 5 (founder review round, continued): founder caught both journal images rendering vertically stretched — the page's image reset lacked height:auto, so width-constrained images kept their literal height attributes. Fixed at the root here and across five sibling sites carrying the same latent bug, both journal photographs now display as true squares (the walk is a centre-square crop of the keeper) per founder direction, every image on all eleven portfolio sites was measured programmatically for displayed-vs-natural aspect at desktop and 390 px (zero violations), and the lesson became a permanent repo test so it cannot recur.
