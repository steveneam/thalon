How this page was made

Loopwell is a fictional demonstration business. Nothing on the main page is a real product, price, or customer result; the charts show sample data from an invented workspace, labeled as such where they appear. This site is one entry in a portfolio of design templates, each built for a different kind of business.

The design idea

A usage-analytics product is an instrument, so the page is built as one: a strip-chart recorder on white millimeter paper. The whole page uses a strict three-ink system, like a printed instrument manual: paper white, cobalt ink for everything structural, and one signal red that is reserved for live or measured marks. If something on the page is red, it is data.

The hero takes the idea literally. The recorder at the top of the page measures your own visit (elapsed time, scroll, pointer travel) and draws it as the trace, entirely inside your browser. This page makes no network calls from its scripts, and the trace is never transmitted or stored.

Type and materials

  • Archivo (variable): display and body. Set wide and black for headlines, like bezel engraving.
  • Martian Mono (variable): readouts and labels. Tabular numerals for live figures.
  • Both typefaces are open-source under the SIL Open Font License and are served from this site, not a third-party host. Their license texts ship in the site's fonts/ directory.
  • No frameworks, no trackers, no external requests: hand-written HTML, CSS, and one small script for the instrument.

Build notes: iteration passes

The page went through repeated full passes, each hunting faults (layout, contrast, motion honesty, accessibility) and pushing one deliberate ambition item.

  1. PASS 1 — Fault lane: below-fold charts could render undrawn when reveal animation was gated on a scroll observer; charts are now fully visible by default and the draw-in is a pure enhancement (same guarantee for no-script visits). Copy cadence tightened; navigation button wrap fixed on small screens. Ambition lane: the recorder now runs a power-on calibration sweep (a pen check), so the strip has character before you have moved at all.
  2. PASS 2 — Fault lane: the sample event stream mixed fixed seed timestamps with live clock rows (seeds now retire when live rows flow); the closing photograph's crop keeps the pen in frame on narrow screens; the band is knitted to the ink system with a hairline rule. Ambition lane: the instrument closes the loop. The closing band reports your own visit back to you, beside the ask.
  3. PASS 3 — Fault lane: full breakpoint walk (phone, tablet, desktop), contrast sweep on every text-over-color pairing, reduced-motion and no-script states verified honest. Ambition lane: ceiling call. The page's one memorable thing is the live recorder; anything further would be noise, so nothing was added.

Honesty rules this page follows

  • No invented testimonials, customer logos, or usage statistics presented as real.
  • No fake urgency or scarcity.
  • Sample data is labeled as sample data.
  • The fictional nature of the business is disclosed (this page, and the footer).
