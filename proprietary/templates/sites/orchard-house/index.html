Orchard House

A seasonal kitchen in a working orchard. Forty-two trees, one long table, and a menu that follows them through the year.

It begins in autumn ↓
I. Autumn

The Harvest

The year’s work comes in. Crates stack by the cellar door, the presses run, and the kitchen cooks with its arms full.

From the autumn card
Roast pumpkin, brown butter, sage26
with hazelnuts from the far fence line
Slow duck, roasted quince, cider jus38
the quinces poached in last year’s cider
Apple galette, orchard cream18
five varieties, one pan
From the rows: apples, quinces, walnuts. The cellar fills.
II. Winter

The Rest

The trees sleep and the pantry speaks. What autumn put away — preserved, pickled, cellared — carries the table through the cold.

From the winter card
Celeriac soup, toasted rye, cultured butter22
cellar vegetables, kitchen bread
Braised beef cheek, mash, pickled walnut36
the walnuts pickled green last spring
Preserved-quince crumble, bay custard17
The orchard rests. The pruning is done by hand; the jars come down by the dozen.
III. Spring

The Blossom

Three weeks of blossom, and the whole hill hums. The kitchen goes light and green while the bees work overtime.

From the spring card
Asparagus, poached egg, blossom honey24
honey from the hives between the rows
Lamb shoulder, first peas, mint34
Rhubarb and custard tart16
orchard eggs, first-pull rhubarb
Petal-fall decides the crop. We count hives, not hours.
IV. Summer

The Long Table

Tables under the tree from December to March. Lunch runs long, the light runs longer, and the first apples land before the plates are cleared.

From the summer card
Tomatoes, torn bread, basil oil21
grown against the north wall
Chicken off the coals, charred lemon33
Stone fruit, almond, vanilla ice16
peaches and apricots from the neighbour’s rows
Everything at its ripest; nothing out of season. The year turns again.
Visit

Orchard House, Milldale Lane, Adelaide Hills

Lunch Thursday to Sunday from noon. Dinner Friday and Saturday. Closed on frost mornings — call ahead in July.
