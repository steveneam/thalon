## Look-first taste notes (Dribbble roofing · Dribbble brutalist · Pinterest storm/materials)

1. **Corrugation IS the grid.** Drone-down Colorbond ribs are ready-made
   brutalist structure — the page's rules and columns derive from roof
   anatomy (ridge = chapter spine-rule, valleys = literal gutter channels
   between columns), never decoration.
2. **The sky is the antagonist and the palette.** Storm supercells run
   teal-charcoal with ember underlight; roofing exists BECAUSE of weather,
   so the drama is honest. The loud brutalist accent is terracotta oxide
   against storm monochrome.
3. **Type as signage, in roofing's own language.** Giant cropped all-caps
   display + dense spec strips — but the data is pitch°, gauge mm, span,
   AS-4055-style wind class. Material-spec brutalism, constructed to be
   UNLIKE Crateline's paperwork-manifest register: ⑯ carries NO documents;
   its data lives stamped on material and in one storm instrument.
4. **Material macro carries the cinematic.** Wet corrugation, raindrops on
   tile, seam lines in storm light — tactile, premium, zero-people frames
   with structurally zero pseudo-text risk (continuous surfaces).
5. **Refused defaults (named, 15+ sightings):** "Your Trusted Partner in
   Quality Roofing Solutions" headline class · navy/orange SaaS template +
   3D house render · camera-facing hi-vis thumbs-up crew · "500+ roofs"
   stat counters · icon service card-grids. Also refused: the adjacent trap
   of sticker-maximalism wearing the brutalist name.

## The concept — the page is a ROOF UNDER WEATHER

The page is built like a roof, and a storm arrives over the course of the
scroll. Brutalism = the structure: every chapter opens under a load-bearing
RIDGE rule, columns separate along VALLEY channels, spec plates are stamped
into surfaces, **zero border-radius anywhere** (the portfolio's only fully
square site — anti-polish discipline as identity), ink on zinc-white with
one oxide accent. Cinema = the weather: the sky layer runs clear noon →
bruised supercell → burst → washed evening light on the scroll clock, and
minted storm/material photography carries the emotional register.

**The one clock, two readings (instrument + lay twin):** a code-drawn
STORM GAUGE (mm/h rain · km/h gusts climbing toward the roof's stated wind
class) runs beside a code-drawn ROOF CROSS-SECTION (ridge cap, battens,
sarking, gutter, downpipe) that visibly SHEDS the same storm — rain streaks
hit the pitch, run the fall, and the downpipe pulses. The gauge earns the
expert's trust; the section shows everyone else what a roof is FOR.

**The wink:** at storm peak, one small card gets a blue emergency TARP
pinned over its corner — "temporary fix · book the permanent one." The
tarp is roofing's most human artifact. Visible statically in no-JS.

**Nature-vs-dread (§8):** roofing's dread is the leak and the storm bill;
the close answers the fear — the same roofline calm in washed evening
light, CTA: "Book an inspection before the season does it for you"
(honest, no fake urgency).

## Chapters

1. **HERO** — full-bleed storm-front-over-corrugated-roofline mint; display
   type "RIDGE & VALLEY" with the R cropped off the page edge; spec plate
   (code-drawn) stamps pitch/gauge/wind-class. No people.
2. **THE ANATOMY** — the code-drawn cross-section, labelled like a
   technical plate; copy: what a re-roof actually replaces.
3. **MATERIALS** — three bands (corrugated · terracotta tile · standing
   seam), macro mint + spec strip + honest colour-chip row (generic names:
   Oxide, Basalt, Zinc, Gum). Consumable-result rule: the material IS the
   product; chips show what the customer's house wears.
4. **THE STORM TEST** — sky at peak; gauge + section run together; rain
   streaks the viewport; the tarp wink appears.
5. **THE CREW** — one mint: early-sixties roofer, silver stubble, plain
   dark waffle-knit (worn-fabric rule), fitting ridge capping, absorbed,
   three-quarter, clearing sky (casting archetype §7: trades lead older
   male, wisdom is the signal).
6. **AFTER THE STORM** — the hero roofline calm in evening light (causal
   pair with the hero, ⑮ handoff pattern; continuity DIRECTED not literal —
   /guide discloses); CTA + footer + /guide.

## No-JS / reduced-motion

The storm RESOLVES: no-JS and reduced-motion get the complete page with the
sky as static per-chapter bands, the gauge at its peak reading, rain as
static streak texture in chapter 4, and the tarp visible. Scroll clock is a
JS enhancement (rAF over transform/opacity); CSS static fallback is the
default markup state.

## Decision annotations

- All in-scene text code-drawn → kills the soul pseudo-text class for this
  site entirely; spec plates are HTML, not mints.
- Text panels never sit on the changing sky directly — content rides solid
  paper/ink plates so AA holds at every storm stage by construction.
- Zero border-radius = the anti-polish discipline done ON PURPOSE once in
  the portfolio; paired with generous whitespace so raw ≠ cramped.
- Storm = the one clock; no second animation system exists.
- Distinct-from-Crateline is structural: no waybills, no ledgers, no
  stamps-as-paperwork — data lives on material spec plates + one instrument.
