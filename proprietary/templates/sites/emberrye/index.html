The Neighborhood Table House notes from Ember & Rye — no. 3, winter

The oven decides.

Notes from our kitchen on why the menu is short, the bread is dark, and the fire is the only clock we keep.

We light the oven at five and it tells us what kind of night it will be. Some evenings it runs hot and fast and wants fish whole in fig leaf, blistered and done in minutes; some evenings it settles low and long and the lamb shoulder goes in early and comes out at eight, sighing. We stopped arguing with it years ago. The menu is written each morning from what the market had and what the fire will agree to, which is why it fits on one board and changes more often than the weather.

If the oven won’t give it, it isn’t on the menu.— house rule, unbroken

The rye is the other constant. A dark, cross-scored loaf raised on a starter older than our lease, baked in the oven’s falling heat after the last service — which is to say tonight’s dinner bakes tomorrow’s bread. It comes to every table first, with cultured butter, whether you ordered it or not. Neighbors argue about whether the first slice or the heel is the better one. We stay out of it.
