How this page was made

Ember & Rye is a fictional demonstration business. No real restaurant, no real address, no real menu — the prices are sample prices and the board is invented. This site is one entry in a portfolio of design templates, each built for a different kind of business.

The design idea

A restaurant built around one wood-fired oven has a natural story: a single evening, lit by a single fire. So the page is shot and cut like a short film — full-bleed stills carry it, the copy recedes into subtitle captions and timecodes, and scrolling plays the night from 17:05 (the fire is lit) to 23:58 (the last embers). The page itself color-grades as you go: a fixed dusk tint dissolves into an ember one, and each frame’s timecode warms along the same arc, so the scroll bar becomes the clock of the evening — read the little timecode rail on the right.

Halfway through, the film is interrupted by print: the menu arrives as a magazine insert — double rules, a drop cap, a grid-breaking photograph, dot-leader dishes — because a neighborhood restaurant’s other natural document is the thing pinned to its door. The insert is printed night-side: dark stock and cream ink rather than bright paper, so the page never breaks its low light mid-scroll (a direction from review — the one bright object left is the bread photograph, tipped in on a cream mat). It is the restaurant’s own house notes, not a pretend press review; nobody is quoted saying it’s wonderful.

Type and materials

  • Fraunces (variable, with true italics): the masthead, editorial headlines, long-form text and menu dishes — one optical-size axis covers the whole print voice.
  • Space Grotesk (variable): timecodes, kickers, captions and the practical band — the film-credit voice.
  • Both typefaces are open-source under the SIL Open Font License and served from this site; their license texts ship in the site’s fonts/ directory.
  • No frameworks, no third-party code, no trackers, and no network calls from any script: what happens on this page stays on this page.

Imagery

Every photograph on the page is AI-generated, minted for this site on a paid, commercially-licensed plan — seven cinematic stills from one model, prompted as a single evening with one consistent grade (the magazine’s bread photograph is deliberately print-graded instead). Full provenance for every image (model, prompt, spend, license tier) is recorded in the studio’s asset register at mint time. One take was rejected and re-shot for a badly drawn hand; its spend is recorded too.

Build notes: iteration passes

The page went through repeated full passes, each hunting faults (layout, contrast, motion honesty, accessibility) and pushing one deliberate ambition item.

  1. PASS 1 — Fault lane: the hero’s timecode chip collided with the navigation (moved to the bottom of the frame, above the letterbox bar); every chapter caption sat left of center — two centering transforms were stacking (rebuilt on margins); the magazine photograph’s grid-break overshot its column gap and touched the text (pulled back); the timecode rail was invisible over the print insert, leaving an orphaned glowing dot. Ambition lane: the letterbox bars now retract as the night begins — the frame opens like a shutter over the first scroll.
  2. PASS 2 — Fault lane: the rail’s backdrop pill (pass-1’s fix) read as a grey lozenge on the insert and covered the menu prices at tablet width — replaced with the honest move: the film chrome fades out entirely while the print insert is on screen, and the rail stays desktop-only; the stacked magazine photograph ballooned to full column width (constrained to editorial scale). Ambition lane: the timecodes now run — while a frame holds the screen its clock ticks forward a minute at a time, so the film is playing, not paused.
  3. PASS 3 — Fault lane: full breakpoint walk (390/768/1024/1440); at phone width the wordmark wrapped into the navigation and the letterbox credit overflowed its bar (nowrap, trimmed nav, the credit yields before it wraps); a computed contrast sweep found two pairings under AA — the letterbox credit and the fictional-address fine print — both lightened and re-measured; keyboard walk (logical order, visible focus everywhere, skip link lands on chapter one); no-script and reduced-motion states verified honest (full page visible, static grade, no rail, no ticking). Mid-build direction from review: the insert was originally bright cream — re-printed as the midnight edition so the page never breaks its low light. Ambition lane: ceiling call — the running clock, the dusk-to-ember grade and the dark-stock insert are this page’s memorable moments; anything further would be noise.

Honesty rules this page follows

  • No invented testimonials, review scores, awards or press quotes — the magazine insert is the fictional restaurant’s own voice, labeled as house notes.
  • No fake urgency or scarcity; the only clock on the page is the fiction of one evening, and the timecodes say what they are.
  • The menu is labeled a sample; the address is labeled fictional where it appears.
  • The fictional nature of the business is disclosed (this page, the magazine folio, the practical band and the footer).
