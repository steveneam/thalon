How this page was made

Tsukimi (月見) is a fictional demonstration business. This page is a portfolio template — it shows how a Japanese restaurant's landing page can be designed, written, and illustrated by an AI-driven content engine. No dish, price, seating, or address on it is real.

The design

The page is one night. Its signature is an exceptional colour palette: four courses, each a single ingredient drenched in one saturated colour — crimson, amber, green, violet — glowing out of near-black, arced across the meal. The one moving element is the moon: a code-drawn disc that crosses a fixed night sky as you scroll (moonrise to moonset), which is both the cinematic clock and the page's nature motif. Between the colours sits one held chapter with a single line from the kitchen, set over a dim lantern-lit view of the counter and the open kitchen behind it — the pacing of a menu read slowly. Without JavaScript, or with reduced motion enabled, the moon simply rests at mid-sky and every course renders in full. There's a small joke in the moon, too: look into it and you'll find the rabbit pounding mochi — the legend the restaurant's name comes from. Every word, number, the moon, the phase marker, and the rabbit are HTML and SVG, not images.

The imagery — all AI-generated

Every photograph on this page was generated with the Higgsfield Soul 2.0 model; nothing here is a stock photo, and no photograph from the web appears on this page. The page first shipped as a fine-dining tasting menu; in a founder-directed revision it was re-themed to an everyday Japanese kitchen (ramen, sushi, soba, rice) and the four course photographs were re-shot — close, low-angle, glistening — this time guided by reference images: public photographs (largely Wikimedia Commons) were given to the model as generation references, so it could see exactly the dish, angle and light we wanted. The references only steer the model; the images it returns are new. Two takes were rejected for colour drift (a broth too pale, green tea noodles that came out grey) and re-shot; the green course that made the cut was a pure text prompt. One earlier honest note, kept for the record: the original crimson course was planned as tuna but minted as beetroot, and we captioned it as beetroot rather than pretend; with a real nigiri reference this revision finally shot the tuna. The amber course is cropped at its right edge to remove a decorated spoon the model added on its own.
