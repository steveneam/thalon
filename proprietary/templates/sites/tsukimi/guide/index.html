How this page was made

Tsukimi (月見) is a fictional demonstration business. This page is a portfolio template — it shows how a fine-dining restaurant's landing page can be designed, written, and illustrated by an AI-driven content engine. No dish, price, seating, or address on it is real.

The design

The page is one night. Its signature is an exceptional colour palette: four courses, each a single ingredient drenched in one saturated colour — crimson, amber, green, violet — glowing out of near-black, arced across the meal. The one moving element is the moon: a code-drawn disc that crosses a fixed night sky as you scroll (moonrise to moonset), which is both the cinematic clock and the page's nature motif. Between the colours sits one held black chapter with a single line from the kitchen — the pacing of a real tasting menu. Without JavaScript, or with reduced motion enabled, the moon simply rests at mid-sky and every course renders in full. There's a small joke in the moon, too: look into it and you'll find the rabbit pounding mochi — the legend the restaurant's name comes from. Every word, number, the moon, the phase marker, and the rabbit are HTML and SVG, not images.

The imagery — all AI-generated

The six photographs were generated with the Higgsfield Soul 2.0 model, one take each. No stock, no scraped references. One honest note: the first course was planned as tuna, but the generated image read clearly as glazed beetroot rounds (you can see the beet's growth rings), so we changed the course to match the picture — beetroot cured in plum — rather than caption an image as something it isn't.
