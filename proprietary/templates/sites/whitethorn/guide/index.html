How this page was made

Whitethorn is a fictional demonstration business. It has no premises, no staff and no patients. Every photograph on the landing page is AI-generated, the recovery it shows is a composite rather than a record, and this note explains the method honestly.

What is real and what is not

  • The practice is invented. The address, the hours, the two-vet structure and the out-of-hours arrangement are all illustrative.
  • Bracken is a composite. No single animal is being described. The eighty-four-day arc, the day numbers and the figures are written to be typical of the injury, not transcribed from a case file.
  • The lameness scale is real. Lameness is scored 0–5 in clinics every day, and marker-based gait analysis on a treadmill is a real veterinary technique. We used a true scale rather than inventing one, because an invented scale is exactly the dishonesty a page like this is most exposed to.
  • The dip is deliberate. The numbers get worse at Day 12, after the operation, because that is what actually happens. A recovery graph that only ever rises would have been the easier page and the less truthful one.
  • No testimonials, no counters, no urgency. There is no "500 happy patients" strip anywhere on the page, because we would have had to make it up.

The design

The page is one idea: a walk that gets better. Its central claim — that a practice can return an animal to the life it had — is never asserted in a sentence. It is demonstrated by an instrument you scroll.

The gait study pins to the side of the screen while its six chapters scroll past and drive it. Scroll position sets the day, the lameness score, the gait and the speed; from those, everything else follows on one clock — the limb positions, the loop each paw traces through a full cycle, the pelvic hike as the sore leg loads, the marks left on the belt behind, and the four readouts. There is no second animation system anywhere on the site. A page about measured movement should not itself be busy.

The affected limb — the right hind — is drawn in haw red, and haw red is used for nothing else. Every live reading on the page is that colour, so the accent means one specific thing: this number is being measured.

Why the diagram is a diagram. A first attempt drew a naturalistic dog and it read as a bad cartoon, which would have cheapened the whole page. A gait study, though, genuinely is a drawing in veterinary practice: markers on the joints, a belt underfoot, trajectories plotted. Building the honest version of the instrument turned out to be both truer and better-looking than an illustration of one. The photographs carry the feeling; the diagram carries the evidence.

Without JavaScript, or under reduced motion, nothing is lost: every chapter carries its own day, lameness score, symmetry and weight-bearing figures in the markup, so the whole recovery is readable as a static essay.

The imagery

Five photographs, all generated with Higgsfield Soul 2.0 at 2K and pinned with full provenance manifests (model, prompt, credits, licence tier). Fourteen images were generated in total and five were kept — 1.68 credits across the whole site.

Two rejections worth explaining, because both are honest limits of the tool rather than accidents:

  • The yard gate came back as a grid of bars, twice. That image exists to say the opposite of confinement, so a lattice across it quietly reintroduced the exact feeling the section is there to dispel. The fix was to change what is in the frame — a brick archway with the door swung flat open — rather than to keep asking for "no bars".
  • The vet arrived wearing a lanyard with a badge, twice, and the badge carried invented pseudo-lettering. Asking for "no lanyard" did not remove it; cropping the frame at the collarbone did. Image models add small printed surfaces to scenes that imply them, and the reliable fix is to compose the surface out of shot.

Continuity is directed, not literal. The dog in the closing photograph is not pixel-identical to the dog in the hero — the coat markings differ. The two frames were composed as a pair in register and intent rather than as continuous footage, and we would rather say so here than quietly hope nobody looks twice.

The type

Petrona for display and long text, Familjen Grotesk for the body, DM Mono for every instrument reading. All three are open-licensed (SIL OFL) and served from this site's own files — the page loads nothing from anywhere else. Licences ship alongside the fonts.

What the page deliberately refuses

Veterinary landing pages converge hard on one look: mint-and-teal, a retriever staring down the lens, a folded-arms clinician in scrubs, a row of counted-up statistics, and a headline in the family of "Your Trusted Partner in Veterinary Care". This page refuses each of those by name. No teal appears anywhere on it, no subject meets the camera, and the only numbers on the page are the ones the instrument is actually measuring.
