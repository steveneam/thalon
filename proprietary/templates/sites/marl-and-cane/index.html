It is all in there

Everything the wine will ever be is already inside the fruit — the colour, the sugar, the acid, the tannin. Nothing gets added later.

One

So we break it

Whole bunches into concrete, and the weight of the fruit above does most of the work. No press, no pumping, no hurry.

Two

A year of weather,
in a puddle

What runs out is not wine yet. Everything that happened outside last summer is dissolved in it, and none of it can be changed now.

Three

Then we wait

Wild ferment, eighteen months in old oak, no fining and no filtering. The cellar is cold and nothing is asked of it.

Four

And you open it

Dark, dusty and firm for two years, and then it lets go. Cellar door Friday to Sunday. When it is gone we wait a year, like everyone else.

The 2024 ripening record

Sugar up, acid down,
and the fortnight where they cross

We would have picked on the 22nd. It rained. Wet fruit is dilute fruit, so we waited four days and paid two tenths of acid for them — which is the whole vintage, really.

The half that never changes

Cold soil buys time

Marl warms slowly and holds its water deep, so the vine is never in a hurry. The sugar climbs at a walk instead of a run, and the acid is still there when the flavour arrives.

It is why our window opens a fortnight after everyone else's, which has caused arguments.
