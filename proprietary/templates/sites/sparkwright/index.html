The board, read as a menu

Every job leaves a labelled breaker.

No call-out menu of tricks — just the work a house actually needs, each circuit named, tested, and written on the board in a hand you can read.

  1. C-01

    Rewires — full and partial

    Old rubber and cloth wiring out, new cable in, walls patched behind us. Staged so you live at home the whole time.

    You get: a house that passes any inspection you throw at it, and insurance that stays valid.
  2. C-02

    Switchboard upgrades

    Fuse boxes and sun-perished boards replaced with safety switches on every circuit — labelled properly, photographed, documented.

    You get: a board your next electrician will silently thank you for.
  3. C-03

    Fault-finding

    The speciality. The light that flickers only on rainy Tuesdays, the breaker that trips at 6 pm, the warm patch on the wall. We don't guess; we measure until it confesses.

    You get: the actual cause, fixed — not a part swapped on a hunch.
  4. C-04

    Safety switches & smoke alarms

    RCDs tested to trip in milliseconds, interconnected photoelectric alarms, and a written record of both.

    You get: the 30 ms that makes a fault an anecdote instead of an ambulance.
  5. C-05

    Lighting, power & kitchens

    Circuits planned for how you actually live: benches that run the kettle, toaster and air-fryer at once, dimmers that don't buzz, light where you read.

    You get: rooms you want to be in after dark.
  6. C-06

    EV chargers, sheds & outdoors

    Dedicated charger circuits sized against your board's real headroom, weatherproof garden power, sensor lights that only mind their own business.

    You get: a car that's full every morning, on the cheap overnight rate.
  7. C-07

    Mystery switches

    Diagnosed and either given a job or retired with honour. Every house has one; three in ten stay unsolved industry-wide. Ours is better than that.

Work you'll never see

Wired like it will be seen.

Most of our work disappears behind a wall within the week. That's exactly why it's done neatly: the next person to open that wall — in five years or thirty — reads the work like a note left for them.

Runs combed flat and clipped at even spacing. Junctions where the drawing says they are. A board labelled in plain words, not scribble. None of it makes the lights brighter. All of it makes the house honest.

It's the same reason the van is tidy and the boots come off at the door: how you do the small things is how you do the wiring.

Sparkwright is one person, on purpose. The master electrician who quotes your job runs the cable, terminates the board, tests every circuit, and signs the certificate — one pair of hands, one standard, twenty-two years in.

No apprentice learning on your switchboard. No subcontractor you've never met. If the phone rings at ten past nine on a Sunday because the power's out and the fridge is warming, the voice that answers is the one that fixes it.

The morning after

Then you forget about us. That's the job.

The best review an electrician can get is silence: the kettle just boils, the shower is hot, the garden lights come on at dusk and mind their own business, and nobody thinks about the switchboard at all.

Before the van leaves, every circuit is tested and the certificate of electrical safety is in your inbox. After that, the house simply works — mornings like this one.
