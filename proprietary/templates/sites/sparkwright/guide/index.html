How this page was made

Sparkwright Electrical is a fictional demonstration business — a portfolio piece showing what this content factory produces. No real electrician, licence, or suburb address is being advertised; the licence number, establishment year, and place names are plausible fiction written to show how a real trade practice would live on a page like this. All imagery is AI-generated.

Concept

A design survey of the electrician genre found the same defaults everywhere: emergency-green or black-and-safety-yellow palettes, hi-vis workers grinning or gripping cables straight at the camera, stat counters (759+ jobs!), and 24-HOUR banners shouting urgency. This page refuses all of it. The trade's actual product is calm — a house where everything simply works — and its actual visual language, the electrical plan with its symbols and labelled board, is beautiful and almost never used.

So the page is a re-energize job: it opens at blue-hour dusk and scrolling flips a house back on, circuit by circuit. A wireframe house drawn in the electrician's own plan register (luminous edges on night blue) gains warm light room by room, while a code-drawn switchboard flips each breaker on the same scroll clock — the instrument for those who read boards, the glowing house for everyone else. One clock, two readings. The page background itself brightens chapter by chapter, closing on a sunlit morning kitchen: what the customer actually buys.

Design axes (assigned): high-quality-3D primary — the CSS-3D house, energized rather than assembled — with otherworldly animation secondary (the light itself spreading through the model and the page). Type: Bricolage Grotesque, Public Sans, IBM Plex Mono — all OFL, served locally. Zero external requests; no analytics.

The honest instrument

  • The switchboard is real markup, not a picture — its breaker labels, amp ratings, and load readout are the accessible twin of the 3-D scene, and a screen-reader description narrates the same replay.
  • The TEST button does what a real safety switch test does: everything drops, then re-arms — because the 30 milliseconds is the entire point of an RCD.
  • Breaker C7 is labelled MYSTERY and never switches on: every house has the hallway switch nobody can explain. The services list gives it a row too. It stays unsolved on this page on purpose.
  • Without JavaScript (or with reduced motion) the house arrives fully energized, every breaker on, the readout at full load — the finished job, already told.
  • No fake urgency, no invented testimonials, no job counters. The genre's 24/7 scream appears once, quietly, as a fact: faults don't keep office hours.

The portrait took five takes, and the reasons are a faithful record of what image models do to tradespeople: take 1 stared straight into the camera and grew garbled markings on the lamp; take 2 looked away correctly but invented a lettered brand badge on the chest pocket; take 3 pinned an ornate gold brooch to the work shirt; take 4 added three athletic-brand stripes to the sleeve. The keeper swapped the shirt for a waffle-knit jumper — knitted texture resists invented branding — and came back clean. The first cable photograph was rejected because it showed coiled storage, not craft; the keeper's export is cropped toward the hands (recorded in the asset manifest, deterministic from the pinned original). The morning kitchen's shadowed shelf clutter was inspected at 3× zoom for invented lettering before keeping — the two label-like patches read as texture, not text, and were left untouched. Every reject is logged with its provenance; total spend 1.08 credits including rejects.

Method + passes

Built as one self-contained HTML file: the house is CSS 3-D transforms (no engine, no library), the switchboard is plain markup, the replay is ~80 lines of dependency-free JavaScript. The markup ships in the finished, fully-lit state and JavaScript takes it dark to run the replay — so the page degrades to the completed job, never to darkness. Iteration passes (fault-hunt + ambition lanes each) are logged here as they run:

  • Pass 1 — desktop, both lanes. Fault-hunt: the hero's layout column was accidentally sized to its content (fixed with an explicit grid column); the 3-D model's ridge rendered as a detached floating line and the house had no roof — a full pitched gable was built (two slopes, two gable triangles, a glowing ridge edge); the floor-plan room labels rendered mirrored because the rooms plane showed its back face (plane flipped, rows remapped); two rooms contradicted the facade windows (swapped); the hot-water and air-conditioning circuits had no visible story (became framed fixture units on the camera-facing wall); windows floated as amber cards (given frames and muntin crosses); the safety-switch test left breakers visually on while everything else went dark (the trip now drops the whole board, then re-arms circuit by circuit). Ambition: the porch light got a cone down the facade and a pool of light on the ground at C6.
  • Pass 2 — 390 px, both lanes. Fault-hunt: the scene header overlapped the roof and the footer caption collided with the panel (the mobile scene was restructured into normal flow — header above, house one step smaller, caption hidden, panel compacted); the navigation labels wrapped mid-word (under 560 px only Book remains); an orphaned last word in the after-hours line (balanced wrapping). Verified: no horizontal scroll at exactly 390, and the TEST button replay works end-to-end on the phone layout.
  • Pass 3 — final sweep. Without JavaScript the scroll track would have been five empty screens: a noscript style now folds it to a static, fully-energized scene (the markup ships complete; JavaScript takes it dark to run the replay) and hides the TEST button. Keyboard walk (skip link, nav, focus ring on TEST), contrast spot-checks in both colour worlds, console clean, zero external requests, and the squint test against the other fourteen portfolio sites — nothing else is a blueprint-wireframe house with a live switchboard. Ceiling call: the replay plus the trip test is the page's one loud moment; the hero stays deliberately still.

Provenance
