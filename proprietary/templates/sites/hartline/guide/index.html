How this page was made

Hartline Estates is a fictional demonstration business. Nothing on the landing page is a real listing, address, or claim about a real company. Every photograph is AI-generated — commissioned for this page from image models on a paid license tier, with full provenance (model, prompt, credits, license) recorded for each asset at the moment it was generated. The drawings, the 3D model, and the code are original work, written by hand for this page.

The design brief

One single-page landing site for a boutique residential estate agency, built as one of a portfolio of demonstration sites in which every page commits to a different signature technique. This page drew high-quality 3D as its primary direction and soft/organic as its secondary.

Before any design work, published real-estate and architecture sites were studied for what the vertical defaults to — and what to refuse. The luxury default (charcoal, gold serif, an empty glass villa at dusk) photographs money, not home; this page takes the opposite register: warm daylight, limewash and clay, a human-scale house wrapped in greenery. The one honest 3D artifact in real estate is the dollhouse cutaway that explains a home, so the page's 3D is a house that assembles from its own floor plan. The arch — doorway, window, threshold — carries the soft/organic direction.

The 3D, honestly

The scroll scene ("Every home we take on gets taken apart first") is built from no 3D engine at all: the house is ~40 CSS planes — floor, hinged walls, folding roof panels, gable ends, a chimney box — driven by perspective transforms and a scroll-linked camera. Physics/WebGL engines were considered and rejected: there is nothing to simulate, and architecture is made of planes, which CSS renders crisply at zero dependency weight. The model deliberately reads as an architect's presentation model — a drawing that inks in as it rises — while the photography above and below it carries the real-world register.

Motion discipline: nothing on the page moves at idle. The model moves only with scroll, cards tilt only on hover, and the window lamps come on one by one only as the scene completes. With reduced motion (or without JavaScript) the page serves the assembled house and every word of the copy, statically.

Iteration passes

  • Pass 1 — Model geometry rebuilt after first render (gable ends re-parented to their walls, chimney box corrected, roof panels gated out of the plan view); annotation column width fixed; windows and door given paper covers so the drawing "inks in" as the walls rise. Ambition shipped: the unfolded-elevation plan state.
  • Pass 2 — Art direction: the schematic model was demoted from the hero in favour of commissioned garden photography (a model can't carry a boutique first impression; greenery can), and the closing exterior was re-shot for setting — the house now sits centred in its own gated garden rather than on a street corner. Roof shading separated so the ridge reads; flush roof ends; mobile scene re-anchored. Ambition shipped: the lamps go on one by one, and the model sits on a faint drafting ring.
  • Pass 3 — Full breakpoint walk (390/768/1440), computed contrast sweep (every text pairing on paper ≥ 4.9:1), keyboard walk with visible focus throughout, no-JS and reduced-motion renders verified honest (assembled house, all copy visible), console clean at every stop.

Honesty notes

  • The three "current homes" are illustrative samples and say so on their cards.
  • No urgency, scarcity, or testimonial appears anywhere on the page.
  • The page is self-contained: no external requests, no analytics, no trackers.
