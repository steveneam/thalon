How this page was made

TrueBore Plumbing is a fictional demonstration business. No real services are offered, the phone number is a film-style placeholder, and the rate card is labeled as a sample. This site is one entry in a portfolio of design templates, each built for a different kind of business.

The design idea

Plumbing is applied physics — pressure, gravity, and water's total indifference to your weekend. So the page runs on a real physics engine and one strict color rule: the world is board-formed concrete, ink, and safety yellow, and anything blue is literal water. Water is the antagonist; the trade is the answer.

The hero takes it literally. A joint drips under real gravity while a demo gauge counts the litres, and the gate valve actually turns — two and a half full turns, righty-tighty — until the leak stops and the page stamps your time. The parts bin further down is the same idea played for weight: photographs of six real-looking fittings with honest masses (a 2.6 kg wrench shoves a 120 g coupling, never the reverse) that you can pick up and toss.

Type and materials

  • Anton: display type, set like stencilled site signage.
  • IBM Plex Mono (400/600): body, labels, and the gauges — job-sheet voice.
  • Both typefaces are open-source under the SIL Open Font License and served from this site; their license texts ship in the site's fonts/ directory.
  • Physics: Matter.js 0.20.0 (MIT), vendored locally with its license header intact — the only third-party code on the page.
  • No frameworks, no trackers, and no network calls from any script: what happens on this page stays on this page.

Imagery

The six fitting photographs in the parts bin are AI-generated, minted for this page on a paid, commercially-licensed plan, then isolated onto transparent backgrounds so the physics engine can tumble them. Full provenance for every image (model, prompt, spend, license tier) is recorded in the studio's asset register at mint time.

Build notes: iteration passes

The page went through repeated full passes, each hunting faults (layout, contrast, motion honesty, accessibility) and pushing one deliberate ambition item.

  1. PASS 1 — Fault lane: the headline's yellow plate painted at font-box height, bleeding over the line above and the paragraph below (rebuilt as a glyph-hugging block); the leak-stopped stamp landed on top of the burst gauge (moved onto the pipe, next to the failed joint); the rate card's "no surprises" stamp was clipped by its own scroll container; the ™ rendered full-size. Ambition lane: the headline now surrenders — closing the valve strikes through WINS. and stamps (not today.) beside it.
  2. PASS 2 — Fault lane: the rate card clipped its detail column on phones (it now folds into a job-sheet list); the figure chips crowded the header hairline on small screens; confirmed the parts bin boots at human scroll speed on phones. Ambition lane: impact dust — collisions in the bin puff concrete dust scaled by mass times speed, so the wrench lands visibly heavier than the coupling.
  3. PASS 3 — Fault lane: full breakpoint walk (390/768/1024/1440, no overflow anywhere), contrast sweep on every text pairing (all clear AA), no-script and motion-off states verified honest (static leak, full protocol pipe, parts laid out in a grid), full keyboard walk with visible focus on every control — including the valve's dashed focus ring, which was wired to the wrong sibling and never showed until this pass. Ambition lane: ceiling call — the valve, the surrendering headline and the bin are this page's memorable moments; anything further would be noise.

Honesty rules this page follows

  • No invented testimonials, review scores, or customer logos.
  • No fake urgency or scarcity — the only countdown on the page is the one you control with the valve, and it is labeled a demo.
  • The rate card is labeled as a sample; the gauge is labeled as a demonstration.
  • The fictional nature of the business is disclosed (this page, the closing band, and the footer).
